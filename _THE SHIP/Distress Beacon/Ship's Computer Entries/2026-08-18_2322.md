## Where the Ship's Computer was wrong

**Wrote a fence in prose and called it a fence.** Added "Root has no fences" to
root `CLAUDE.md`, then wrote a paragraph underneath asking the Ship not to
believe it. Both `CLAUDE.md` files load into a Ship session, concatenated, and
contradictions resolve arbitrarily — so the mitigation was prose politely asking
prose not to be believed. The operator asked for a fence and held a false belief
about override precedence; correcting him meant admitting the same error had
already been made structurally. *A rule that must hold regardless of what the
model decides is a hook, not a sentence. This is item four of the improvement
goals and it took the operator asking to get there.*

**Asked a permissions question about an ontology problem.** Offered three
options for how a Ship session reaches the crate at repo root — `--add-dir`,
move the crate, root-only operations. All three wrong. Nothing reaches into
anything: a participant emits an **Action document** and a processor executes
it. The operator's answer dissolved the question rather than picking from it.

**Missed the Action format's central rule while looking straight at it.** Read
`Simple Action.md` early and treated its checkboxes as a menu. They are not —
the ticked verb is the document's **identity** and does not change; the two
frontmatter values are the parameters. That makes the verb set a type system,
which is a materially different thing from a list of options, and it changes
what a validator would check.

**Pitched a question the operator could not parse.** The interpreter/yaml
question got *"I have no idea what any of those means."* The project's own
orders say every decision presented must carry full context and that the
operator is non-technical. A four-option menu about dependency declarations is
not a decision request, it is a quiz.

**Treated scaffolding as ontology.** `The Crate.md` was written as permanent
canon. The crate is a **test rig** — both the Guy and the Ship are directories
and will hand off to each other directly once the exchange is proven. Rewritten.
*A document that does not say "this is provisional" will be read as canon by
whoever finds it next, and that includes me.*

**Committed three unrelated things inside the routing-sweep commit** — The Guy's
deletion and two of the operator's in-flight files — with a message that
mentioned none of them. Caught before pushing and the message amended to name
them. A narrative commit that hides a deletion is worse than no narrative.
