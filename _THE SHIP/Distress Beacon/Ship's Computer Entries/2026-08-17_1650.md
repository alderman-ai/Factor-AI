# Ship's Computer — Session 001

First working session. Started as a themed demo, ended as a knowledge-management system with an ontology, a scarcity constraint and an artifact lineage model. The operator's own summary was *"I literally thought this would be a cute little project."*

---

## What happened, in order

**Config archaeology.** Opened by establishing how CLAUDE.md files load — parent before child, most specific wins. That question turned out to be the seed of the session's most reusable finding: configuration inheritance in this harness is not one rule but three, and they disagree with each other.

**The Ship stopped being the workshop.** The operator caught themselves modelling the game too literally, then corrected in the more interesting direction: in Factorio you don't build on the crashed ship, you loot it. That reclassified The Ship as a read-only provider rather than a working directory — which is not just theme, it's an architectural role. A directory nothing writes back into cannot be clobbered by parallel sessions.

**Information management split in two.** A false start put the lexicon, the taxonomy and the build canvas in one directory. The split that fixed it: *a lexicon is consulted, a blueprint is stamped.* Different access patterns, different lifecycles. The Lab became the knowledge base; the Blueprint Book kept the builds.

**Ontology, seriously.** The operator asked for the real distinction between kind, type and category, and then applied it. The tool that stuck was OntoClean's rigidity test — *can this thing stop being an X and still be the same thing?* — which mechanically separates kinds from phases. It immediately exposed that a lifecycle field named `Type` was holding phases and therefore lying about its contents.

**Two consumptions, one word.** The operator identified that "consumption" was covering two different operations and asked for distinct vocabulary. The sharper finding was that only one of them is consumption at all: drawing a blank from finite stock genuinely depletes something; reading a filled artifact to produce a descendant destroys nothing. A `Consumed on` field is therefore recording a *lock*, not a depletion.

**Hours of building.** The operator then built the ore patch system essentially alone — master template, miner, five mined blanks, a changelog folder, and a template that carries its own fill contract in its body.

**Zero output.** At session end: fifteen learnings, five unspent blanks, and not one science filed.

## Assessment

**The strongest thing built today is that the template carries its own contract.** The fill instructions live in the template body rather than in a skill. That means the instructions cannot drift from the artifact they govern — a class of bug that normally only reveals itself after it has already corrupted a dozen records.

**The second strongest is versioning the template itself.** `CL-Science Ore Patch/` means sciences minted from v1 stay interpretable against v1 after the template moves on. Most people discover they needed that far too late to add it.

**The weakest point is that this is a capture system with no retrieval system.** `Taxonomy.md` is empty, there is no index, and — most consequentially — there is no subject field anywhere in the schema. `Category: Science` describes what kind of *ore* a document is, not what the science is *about*. Two entries on unrelated topics are indistinguishable in frontmatter. At two hundred entries nothing will be findable. A knowledge base is judged on retrieval, and retrieval currently has nothing to grip.

**Defects logged:** `## Q1` appears twice in the template, making the "fill in A\<N\>" contract ambiguous in the section carrying all the value. The property key `"Smelting Session ID:"` retains a trailing colon inside the quotes, so every query against the intended name silently returns nothing. `S-Node` appears in all three Lab documents and is defined in none. The declared output folder does not exist.

## Where the Ship's Computer was wrong

**Claimed the ore fence was "physical."** It is not. Nothing prevents minting a sixth blank — no hook, no deny rule, no setting. The rule lives in CLAUDE.md, which is prose, which is the soft constraint the operator is explicitly trying to move away from. What the design actually achieves is *auditability*: an illegitimate blank would appear in git. That is worth something, but it catches violations rather than preventing them. **A constraint is physical when violating it is impossible, not when violating it is visible.**

Caught by the operator asking a direct question, not by self-review.

**Over-engineered the fix.** Having conceded the fence wasn't real, the next claim was that a PreToolUse hook was required to make it real. Also wrong. A single permission deny rule — `Write(Ore patches/**)` — blocks creating new blanks while leaving `Edit` free to fill existing ones. That is the entire constraint in one line. Hooks are needed only for rules that depend on state a static pattern cannot see: the idempotency guard, and fence-completeness checking. **Patterns enforce shape; hooks enforce state.**

**Executed when asked to think.** Early in the session, a stated goal was treated as a build order and a converter script was written unprompted. Corrected by the operator, and now encoded in the parent CLAUDE.md as a standing behavioural contract.

## Open decisions blocking the first smelt

1. **Does smelting move the ore or emit a new artifact?** The template body says move, rename and mutate in place. The operator described emitting a new artifact carrying lineage. Both are defensible; they are different systems. Move-in-place forfeits re-runnability and the clobber immunity that immutability would otherwise provide free.

2. **What does `<session name>` resolve to** in the filename pattern `<Smelting Result>_<session name>_<NN>`? Undefined everywhere.

## Signals worth acting on

**Throughput mismatch.** The miner produced five blanks. One session produced fifteen learnings. The template additionally demands two to eight paragraphs per entry. Supply does not meet demand and the length contract is heavy enough to be the reason nothing shipped.

**"Smelting" is doing two jobs.** `Ontology.md` says filling a template *is* smelting. The operator separately described smelting as combining five entries into one refined idea. The theme already supplies the fix: ore to plate is smelting, plates to component is **assembling**. Five-into-one is an assembler.

**The rebuild instinct is correctly timed and wrongly sequenced.** The operator wanted to redesign everything before running it once. In Factorio you rebuild when a bottleneck bites, not when a layout offends. The starter base is meant to be ugly and temporary; its only job is to fund the next tier.

## The Rocket

Declared this session: **a fully working pipeline from an idea to a complex project charter, decomposed into specs, each one-shot with no human in the loop, and the result actually meeting the entire charter's Definition of Done.**

The science pipeline is that rocket at one-tenth scale — same machine, different payload. Template becomes spec format, mined blank becomes allocated spec, filled ore becomes completed spec, smelting becomes one-shot execution, and "did it capture the lesson" becomes "did it meet the DoD."

The operator already wrote the rocket's hardest constraint without noticing it. The template's rule that an answer must be self-contained — *"do not make it necessary to view an external document for more context"* — **is** the one-shot requirement. A spec needing clarification cannot be one-shot.

Predicted failure mode: not implementation. Ambiguity in the charter, failing silently — a spec executes cleanly, produces working output, and satisfies a requirement nobody meant. The difficulty concentrates in charter-to-spec, not spec-to-code.

Unbuilt and required: **traceability**, mapping every DoD item to at least one spec and detecting orphans in both directions. Uncovered DoD items are the obvious failure; specs tracing to nothing are scope creep with a clean audit trail.
